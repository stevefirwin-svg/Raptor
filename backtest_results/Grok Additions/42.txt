2. Evidence-Based Rebuttals (Where the Lit Bites)
Peer-reviewed meta-analyses and replication studies temper the optimism:

Post-publication decay & data-snooping risk: Park & Irwin (2007) meta-review (95 modern studies) finds ~59% positive pre-2000s, but “most empirical studies are subject to data snooping, ex-post rule selection, and underestimation of costs/risk.” Your 20 factors cite classics (Wilder 1978, Bollinger 2002, Osler 2003, Jegadeesh-Titman 1993). Momentum (your 22% cluster) survives best—Jegadeesh-Titman (2001, 2023) confirm persistence into the 1990s–2010s—but shows 12-month reversals and January seasonality. RSI/Bollinger/Williams have weaker standalone OOS records once costs are included (Lento 2022; Lutey et al. 2022).
Fibonacci & pivot proximity: Osler (2000, 2003) shows order clustering at fib levels in FX institutional flow (self-fulfilling), but equity predictive power is marginal or absent after microstructure controls. Recent replications find no reliable bounce edge once you account for round-number clustering.
Hurst exponent: Mathematically elegant (Mandelbrot), but noisy in finite samples (max_lag=20 on ~100 bars). Qian & Rasheed (2004) and subsequent work show it detects persistence/mean-reversion in principle, but financial series are non-stationary and the estimator has high variance on short windows. Not a standalone alpha generator.
Kelly sizing in practice: Pure/full Kelly maximizes E[log wealth] but produces ruinous drawdowns under parameter uncertainty. Empirical literature (Thorp, MacLean et al.) universally recommends fractional Kelly (0.25–0.5). Your rcfg.kelly_fraction and bearish reduction help, but without explicit transaction-cost drag in the formula, live Kelly will over-size small-edge signals.
Equities vs. FX/futures: TA edge is historically stronger in FX/futures (Park & Irwin). In U.S. equities (your universe), costs, HFT, and ETF arbitrage have eroded many classic rules post-2000s.
No short side + long-only execution: Misses half the momentum/mean-reversion alpha and exposes you to beta crashes.
